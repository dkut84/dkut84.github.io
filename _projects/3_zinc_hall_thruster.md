## Overview
Conventional Hall effect thrusters rely on scarce and expensive noble gases like Xenon and Krypton. Zinc offers high atomic mass, solid storage density, and low cost, but requires a propellant feed architecture operating above its vaporization temperature without clogging or inducing catastrophic thermal stress on thruster ceramics.

---

## Technical Highlights

### 1. Steady-State Thermal FEA
- Conducted coupled thermal conduction and radiation simulations in **ANSYS** to optimize resistive heater element placement and multi-layer insulation thickness.
- Maintained a strictly uniform **$650^\circ\text{C}$ temperature profile** across the entire vapor transport line, eliminating cold spots that cause metallic zinc condensation and line freezing.

### 2. High-Gradient Thermal Stress Mitigation
- Mitigated mechanical stress induced by severe **$700^\circ\text{C}$ thermal gradients** between the vapor chamber and ambient thruster brackets.
- Engineered specialized **U-bend flexible piping sections** and floating kinematic mounts to absorb differential thermal expansion, safeguarding brittle boron nitride ceramic components.

### 3. Material Compatibility & Metallurgy
- Evaluated high-temperature corrosion mechanisms, establishing that standard aerospace materials (aluminum and stainless steels) degrade catastrophically when exposed to liquid/vapor zinc.
- Designed feed lines using refractory alloys, high-density graphite compression gaskets, and passive vacuum thermal isolation.

### 4. Modular Assembly Architecture
- Partitioned the CAD assembly into three independent modular sub-assemblies:
  1. **Evaporation Reservoir** (crucible and primary heating)
  2. **Vapor Transport Line** (expansion joint and line tracing)
  3. **Multi-Port Distributor** (anode gas manifold)
- Enabled rapid laboratory test swaps for alternative mass flow controllers and heating coils.
