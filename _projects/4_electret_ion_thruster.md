## Overview
Gridded ion thrusters achieve exceptionally high specific impulse ($I_{sp} > 3000\text{ s}$), but ion beam divergence causes grid erosion, thrust vector losses, and spacecraft contamination. This study investigates integrating permanently charged dielectric materials (**electrets**) downstream of the accelerator grid to act as an unpowered, passive electrostatic focusing lens.

---

## Technical Highlights

### 1. Coupled Particle-Field Multiphysics Simulation
- Formulated 2D-axisymmetric electrostatic and charged-particle tracing models in **COMSOL Multiphysics** for the RIT-3.5 thruster grid geometry.
- Solved Poisson’s equation iteratively with space-charge density terms to accurately model ion trajectory deflection under strong mutual Coulomb repulsion.

### 2. Space Thermal & Dielectric Stability Analysis
- Analyzed the thermal limits of fluoropolymer and silicon dioxide electrets under direct radiative solar flux and thruster discharge heat.
- Quantified thermal discharge rates and charge decay half-lives to guarantee dielectric polarization stability throughout nominal mission lifespans.
